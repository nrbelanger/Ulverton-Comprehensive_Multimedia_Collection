History of the Spinning Mule

1779

Invention of the Spinning Mule

Samuel Crompton invented the spinning mule in Bolton, England, combining the spinning jenny and the water frame to produce a finer, stronger thread. This machine was an innovation based on James Hargreaves' Spinning Jenny and Richard Arkwright's Water Frame.

1780-1790

Advancement of Original Design

Refinements of the spinning mule started to be made, such as enhancing the materials used in the machine. Precision and size were both increased in the following years.

1790

Using the Power of Water

Up until this point, the spinning mule was always operated by hand, and there was no way to move the carriage manually moving it. This changed when the spinning mule was altered to be powered by water and steam.

1790-1825

Expansion!

In these years, the functionality of the spinning mules was increased yet again by improving the various aspects of the machine. This included making steam and water applications more efficient, increasing size, and having better tolerance for the components.

1825

Automation!

Richard Roberts patented the first self-acting (automatic) wool spinning mule. This removed the need to move the carriage and allowed for yarn to be produced significantly faster. Before this innovation, the typical mule needed 7 workers to operate efficiently.

1890

Going into the 20th Century

Self-acting wool spinning mules were now the standard and thousands were located across the Canada and the US.

1890-Present

Today's Standard

The ring spinning frame machine gained popularity as it was a more efficient way to manufacture yarn. Over the years, the relevance of ring spinning frame machines overtook wool spinning mules. While the ring spinning frame design is the most common worldwide, rotor spinning machines are a current innovation of this design.
